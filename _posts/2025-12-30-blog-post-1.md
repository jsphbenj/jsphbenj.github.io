I just finished the first semester of my graduate program in City and Regional Planning. Though this year marked a significant change for me in a lot of ways, the transition was as smooth as I could have hoped for.

My Site Planning and Urban Design course scratched an itch for creativity that I didn't know that I had. Given a currently vacant site on Rosemary Street, one of Chapel Hill's two main commercial corridors, we were given the challenge of meeting demand for a new mixed use development on site. To successfully do so, we looked towards contemporary examples of such redevelopment, like nearby Carolina Square. We also learned about the marks history has left on urban form across the country, especially through urban renewal. The site is rooted at the gateway to the Northside neighborhood, a historically Black community continually facing studentification. It was important to design a site that not only served growing housing needs, but also provided open spaces that would be an asset to the existing community. 

The layered requirements of this project was a fun challenge to navigate as I figured out how to maximize both form and function. The involved digging into Chapel Hill's municipal code, making sure that was followed along with the project's square footage requirements for multiple uses and the site's inherent limitations. 

The result of this semester-long team project was "The Sprig on Rosemary". I couldn't have asked for a better group to work with. With this being my first semester in the program, I didn't know my teammates very well when we began working together. Quickly, though, we meshed well and found our strengths in the project. My favorite part was learning and working with SketchUp, the 3D modeling software used for our site renders. I hope to work more on projects that require this type of creativity in the future.
